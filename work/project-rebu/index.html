Zebu · 2015–2018

Project Rebu

A ride-hailing platform that let taxi companies fight back against Uber's rapid expansion.

Role
Founder, CEO & Product Lead
Platforms
Rider and driver apps, desktop dispatch
Funding
Angel investment
Outcome
Live with a taxi company and a limo company, then pivoted

Overview

In 2015 Uber was spreading city by city, and the taxi companies it was eating had no app of their own. Project Rebu gave them one: their own ride-hailing, with the dispatch tools to run it, so a local fleet could compete without turning into an Uber driver pool.

We shipped it. Rebu went live with a taxi company and a limo company, with real riders booking real rides. Then the numbers told us something the pitch deck hadn't, and we pivoted.

Capabilities

  • Rider app: book a ride, watch the car arrive on a live map, pay in the app
  • Driver app: accept a job, navigate to the pickup, close out the trip
  • A desktop dispatch console, so phone bookings and app bookings landed in one queue
  • The fleet's own brand in the app. Riders booked their taxi company, not us
  • Service tiers, so the limo company could run premium bookings on the same platform

Design decisions

  • Fleets first. The paying customer was the taxi company, so the dispatcher's screen got as much care as the rider's.
  • Rebu was the engine, not the name on the car. The fleet kept its brand and its riders.
  • Ship the smallest version that could carry a real passenger, then learn from live rides instead of surveys.

Challenges

  • Selling to an industry that didn't think it had a problem yet.
  • The two-sided cold start: riders want cars on the map, drivers want riders in the app.
  • Fitting a new app into dispatch habits that ran on radios and phone calls.

Wins & losses

  • Raised angel investment to fund development
  • Went live with a taxi company and a limo company, with real riders
  • Customer validation showed taxi companies resisted adoption and didn't see Uber as a threat
  • Saw a weak growth area and pivoted to Zebu to preserve runway
  • Lesson kept: the person who loves the product and the person who pays for it can be different people. Validate both.
