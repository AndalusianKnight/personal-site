Project Rebu

A ride-hailing platform that let taxi companies fight back against Uber's rapid expansion.

Role
Founder, CEO & Product Lead
Platforms
Rider and driver apps, desktop dispatch
Funding
Angel investment
Outcome
Live with a taxi company and a limo company, then pivoted

01The bet

In 2015 Uber was taking cities one at a time, and the taxi companies it was eating had no app of their own. We bet that a local fleet with the same tap-to-ride experience, under its own name, could keep its riders.

02My part

I left university to build it. I founded the company, raised the angel round and led product: the rider app, the driver app, the dispatch console, and every pitch to a fleet owner.

03The hard call

Rebu worked. It went live with a taxi company and a limo company, and real riders booked real rides. But fleet owners kept telling us the same thing: they didn't see Uber as a threat yet, and they didn't want to change how dispatch worked. We could keep selling to an industry that didn't think it had a problem, or stop while we still had runway. We stopped, and moved the team onto Zebu.

04What happened

Two fleets live, real rides, and the clearest lesson of my career: the person who loves the product and the person who pays for it can be different people. Riders loved booking in the app. The owners paying for it needed convincing we couldn't afford to do.

  • 2fleets live
  • 1,400rides booked
  • 7months from idea to first ride

05What I'd do differently

Validate the buyer before building for the user. I'd have spent the first month in fleet owners' offices instead of in the code editor, and found the resistance before we built two apps around it.

Everything it could do
  • Rider app: book a ride, watch the car arrive on a live map, pay in the app
  • Driver app: accept a job, navigate to the pickup, close out the trip
  • A desktop dispatch console, so phone bookings and app bookings landed in one queue
  • The fleet's own brand in the app. Riders booked their taxi company, not us
  • Service tiers, so the limo company could run premium bookings on the same platform
The design rules we kept
  • Fleets first. The paying customer was the taxi company, so the dispatcher's screen got as much care as the rider's.
  • Rebu was the engine, not the name on the car. The fleet kept its brand and its riders.
  • Ship the smallest version that could carry a real passenger, then learn from live rides instead of surveys.
