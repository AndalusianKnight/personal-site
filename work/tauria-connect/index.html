01The bet

Regulated teams were doing their most sensitive work in chat apps their vendors could read. We bet they'd switch to one nobody could read, as long as it felt as good as Slack. A secure tool nobody wants to use just pushes people back to the insecure one.

02My part

I set the rule every feature had to follow (keys never leave the user's devices) and designed the flows that rule makes hard: search, retention, account recovery and password changes, none of which came with a playbook.

03The hard call

Search. Every team asked for it on day one, and it's the one feature encryption makes hard: a server can't index messages it can't read. We could have indexed on the server and been instant, or kept search on each device and kept our promise. We kept the promise. Search ran on the device, and we spent months making it fast enough that people stopped noticing. It was never quite as instant as Slack, and I'd make the same call again.

04What happened

Connect reached close to feature parity with Slack under zero-knowledge encryption, and became the hub the rest of the suite plugged into: files from Lockbox, meetings from Cal, one click into Calls. The moment I remember: a law firm's IT lead telling us it was the first chat app they hadn't needed a security exception for.

  • 150organisations
  • 91%weekly retention
  • 0account-loss incidents

05What I'd do differently

Make bringing a whole team on board the first feature, not a later one. Every new device needs keys of its own, and in early trials that setup ate a team's whole first hour. A team that's still setting up isn't talking yet.

Everything it could do
  • Channels, threads and direct messages, all end-to-end encrypted
  • Search across your messages, without the server ever reading them
  • Message retention policies for compliance teams
  • Files shared through Lockbox, and one click from a thread into a Calls meeting
  • Admin security: device management, login monitoring and suspicious activity alerts
The design rules we kept
  • Feel first. If Connect didn't feel like Slack, the encryption wouldn't matter, because nobody would use it.
  • Keys never leave the user's devices. Every feature was designed around that rule, never around exceptions to it.
  • Explain the encryption inside the product, in plain language, at the moment it matters.
