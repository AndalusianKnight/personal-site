Tauria Cal

Zero-knowledge calendar and scheduling, close to feature parity with Google Calendar.

Role
Founder, CEO & Head of Product
Platforms
Web, desktop, iOS, Android
Benchmark
Google Calendar
Outcome
Acquired with Tauria

01The bet

A calendar says more than people think: who you meet, when, where and about what. We bet that firms who encrypted their files and chats would want their schedule private too, as long as it worked like the calendar they already knew.

02My part

I decided what Cal encrypted (everything that tells a story: titles, notes, places and attendees) and how it worked with people outside Tauria.

03The hard call

Free and busy. To book a meeting you need to know when everyone's free, and the usual answer is a server that reads everyone's calendar. Ours couldn't. We could publish busy times in plain text and keep scheduling simple, or have each device share only free or busy, encrypted, and work out the overlap on the organiser's device. We chose the devices. A colleague could see you were busy at 3, never that it was with a lawyer.

FREEBUSY.EXE

Find a time nobody has to reveal

Four people in four cities need half an hour together. Each device shares only free or busy, encrypted with real AES-256, and the overlap is worked out on the organiser's device. Switch views to see what each side can read, including the server.

04What happened

Close to feature parity with Google Calendar under zero-knowledge encryption, and the glue of the suite: every meeting booked in Cal opened straight into Calls. Our own team, spread across Toronto, The Hague, Belgrade and Lisbon, tested the time zones every single day.

  • 48,000meetings booked
  • 4time zones on our own team
  • 0meeting titles the server could read

05What I'd do differently

Build the integrations first. Calendar standards were made for plain text, so every outside integration became a project. I'd have picked the two that mattered most to customers and built them before the features they asked for less.

Everything it could do
  • Personal and team calendars, end-to-end encrypted
  • Invites, availability and scheduling across time zones
  • A Calls link on every meeting, one click to join
  • Reminders on web, desktop and mobile
  • Import from an existing calendar, so switching didn't mean starting over
The design rules we kept
  • Encrypt everything that tells a story: titles, notes, places and attendees, not just the description.
  • Scheduling with people outside Tauria had to be as easy as sending a link.
  • Time zones done right. A team spread across Toronto, The Hague, Belgrade and Lisbon tested that every single day.
