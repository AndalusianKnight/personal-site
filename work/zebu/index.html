Zebu · 2015–2018

Zebu

A modular ERP for small service businesses: the pivot that led to Tauria.

Role
Founder, CEO & Product Lead
Platforms
Mobile and desktop
Customers
Small service firms
Outcome
Pivoted into Tauria, which raised $5M

01The bet

Small service firms were running on a pile of spreadsheets, a shared calendar, a chat app and a filing cabinet. We bet that one simple system, built in modules, would beat the all-in-one suites that were too big and too expensive for them.

02My part

Founder, CEO and product lead, again. I interviewed the firms, designed the modules and decided what Zebu wouldn't do.

03The hard call

Every industry wanted its own workflow. A cleaning company and an accounting firm both wanted Zebu, as long as we built it their way. Custom builds would have paid the bills and turned us into an agency. We said no and kept the modules general, so a three-person firm and a thirty-person firm could run on the same product.

04What happened

Zebu shipped to real service firms. Then the messaging and file modules raised a question we couldn't ignore: firms kept asking who else could read their clients' data. That question became Tauria, and Tauria raised $5M.

  • 35firms onboarded
  • 5modules
  • 1pivot that raised $5M

05What I'd do differently

Follow the strongest signal sooner. Privacy questions came up in our very first interviews, and I filed them as a feature request. They were the company. I'd have built for them a year earlier.

Everything it could do
  • Clients and jobs in one place, from the first call to the final invoice
  • Scheduling for teams out in the field
  • Quotes, invoices and payment tracking
  • Team messaging and files attached to each client
  • Modules a firm could switch on as it grew
The design rules we kept
  • Modular from day one. A three-person firm and a thirty-person firm shouldn't need the same software.
  • Mobile for the people doing the work, desktop for the office running it.
  • Plain language everywhere. No ERP jargon for people who had never bought an ERP.
